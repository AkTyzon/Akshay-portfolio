Cascade learning on Spark

A cheap model answers everything it is confident about; only the residual escalates to an expensive one. The question worth asking isn't whether the big model is more accurate — it is — but how much of its cost you can avoid without giving that accuracy back.

Sweeping the stage-1 confidence threshold. Higher threshold means stage 1 keeps fewer cases for itself, so accuracy climbs toward the expensive model's — and the saving disappears with it.

What the curve actually says

At a threshold of 0.70 the cascade skips 56% of the expensive model's work and still keeps 98.3% of its accuracy. Push to 0.90 and accuracy is identical to the full model — but 91% of rows now escalate, so there is almost nothing left to save. Drop to 0.50 and the cheap model answers nearly everything, at a real accuracy cost.

That shape is the whole point of a cascade: the useful region is the knee, not either end. Which point you pick is a product decision about how much accuracy a millisecond is worth — not something the model can decide for you.
